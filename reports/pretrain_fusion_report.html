Findings

  • Pretrain-then-fuse is the best model so far, but the gain over the paired-only models is not established. Macro-F1 0.840 ± 0.039 (ROC AUC 0.905) vs 0.777 for the paired-only MLP fusion: +0.063, 95% CI [−0.034, +0.168]. The bootstrap puts 89% of resamples above zero, but 25 participants cannot rule out no difference.
  • The gain comes from the MRI side. The MRI encoder pretrained on 399 scans, frozen, gives 0.784 with a paired-fitted head and 0.744 with no paired fitting at all, matching the paired-only MRI MLP (0.748) without seeing the paired training labels.
  • EEG pretraining did not work. The EEG encoder reaches only 0.55 macro-F1 on its own validation split (≈ chance) with 67 recordings from two sites, and the pretrained EEG model (0.641) is no better than the paired-only EEG MLP (0.664).
  • Fusion vs MRI alone is still not significant (+0.056, CI [−0.066, +0.192]). Fusion vs EEG alone is (+0.199, CI [+0.068, +0.344]).
  • Site confounding in the MRI pool does not drive the result. Restricting the MRI pool to sites with both diagnoses (0.818) or to the paired site CLB only (0.825) gives about the same performance as the full pool (0.840).
  • Stacking the two pretrained heads' logits (0.788) is significantly worse than concatenating embeddings (−0.052, CI [−0.098, −0.012]). Averaging them with no paired fitting (0.787) is on par with the paired-only fusion.
  • Data issue found and handled: one unpaired scan is voxel-identical to the T1 of a paired participant, although the two IDs have different listed ages and sexes. It was removed from the MRI pool, so the pool has 404 scans. Without this check, a test participant's MRI would have been in pretraining.

1. Why this design

Only 25 BrainLat participants have both a usable EEG recording and a T1 MRI, all from one site (CLB). Concatenating EEG and MRI representations needs both modalities for the same person, so the fused model itself can only be fitted and tested on those 25. What the unpaired data can do is train the encoders: each modality's encoder is pretrained on every CN/AD participant that has that modality, then frozen, and only a 17-parameter logistic-regression head is fitted on the paired training fold.

Both pools contain the 25 paired participants, who are removed per split as described below. Several MRI sites contribute only one diagnosis (AR, COA, MXA: CN only; CLA, MXB: AD only), so site and diagnosis are confounded in the full MRI pool; the mixed_sites and clb_only pool variants check how much that matters.

Inputs

  • EEG (26 features): 1–30 Hz, 256 Hz, average reference, ≤300 s, 2 s epochs with bad-channel and bad-epoch rejection; per epoch, relative delta/theta/alpha/beta power and theta/alpha ratio in frontal, central, temporal, posterior and global regions, median over epochs, plus peak alpha frequency. Identical to the paired run (checked: max |difference| = 8.9e-16).
  • MRI (16 features): FreeSurfer 8.2 SynthSeg (--parc --robust) on the T1; 16 AD-signature volumes (hippocampus, amygdala, ventricles, entorhinal, parahippocampal, temporal, precuneus, cingulate, parietal, total cortex and white matter), left+right summed and divided by intracranial volume. Identical to the paired run (max |difference| = 0.0e+00).

4. Training protocol and leakage control

  1. Outer splits: the same 10 × 5-fold stratified splits as the paired-only run (verified fold by fold), so every model below is scored on identical test folds.
  2. For each outer split, the test participants are removed from both pools. The z-scoring, encoders, pretraining heads and fusion head never see a test participant's EEG or MRI.
  3. Pretraining: full-batch AdamW (lr 0.003, 300 epochs), class-balanced cross-entropy. Weight decay ∈ [0.001, 0.01, 0.1] chosen on a stratified 20% validation split of the pool (ties → stronger decay), then the encoder is refitted on the whole pool.
  4. Fusion: encoders frozen; the 16-dim concatenated embedding of each paired participant is z-scored and fed to a class-balanced L2 logistic regression, C ∈ [0.01, 0.1, 1.0] tuned by inner stratified folds on the paired training fold.
  5. Uncertainty: metrics per repeat from out-of-fold predictions (mean ± SD over repeats); model differences by a participant-level stratified bootstrap (2,000 resamples) of the repeat-averaged difference.

6. Limitations

  • The final numbers still come from 25 participants (15 AD, 10 CN) at one site; confidence intervals on differences are wide.
  • The EEG pool is small (72), so the EEG encoder gains much less from pretraining than the MRI encoder.
  • The full MRI pool mixes sites whose diagnosis mix differs; an encoder can partly learn site instead of disease. The site-restricted pools are the check for this.
  • The fusion head's C is tuned on inner folds whose validation participants were in the encoder pretraining pool. This can only bias the choice of C, never the test fold.
  • Pretraining does not create new paired data. This design improves the representations, not the amount of evidence about EEG–MRI complementarity.
